How the rig is built

  • Controls, not clips. Each frame sets where he stands, how far he tips, where his hips are and where his hands and feet go. Knees and elbows are solved by the same two-bone IK the bowler and batter use.
  • Feet on the ground. A running foot is fixed where it lands until it lifts, and the hips drop when a planted leg would be overstretched. Nothing slides.
  • A floor. While he lies after a dive or gets up, the body lifts so no knee or elbow goes into the grass.
  • Planned backwards from the ball. The catcher reads it for 0.23 s, then sprints at no more than 8 m/s and dives the last two metres. He has to come from his own mark.
  • Crossfades. Each action blends out of the one before it, the way an animation mixer blends clips.
